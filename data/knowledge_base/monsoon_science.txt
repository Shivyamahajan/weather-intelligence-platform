INDIAN SOUTHWEST MONSOON — SCIENTIFIC BACKGROUND

Definition:
The Indian Southwest Monsoon is a seasonal reversal of wind direction
that brings moisture-laden air from the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal
over the Indian subcontinent, typically from June to September.

Mechanism:
1. During summer (May-June), the Indian landmass heats up rapidly
2. This creates a low-pressure zone over northwest India
3. The cooler ocean (high pressure) drives moist air toward land
4. As moist air rises over land, it cools and condenses into rainfall

Onset and Withdrawal:
- Normal onset over Kerala: June 1
- Normal onset over Mumbai: June 10
- Normal onset over Delhi: June 29
- Normal withdrawal from northwest India: September 1
- Complete withdrawal from India: October 15

Key Climate Indices Affecting Monsoon:
- El Nino Southern Oscillation (ENSO): El Nino years typically
  bring below-normal monsoon rainfall over India
- Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD): Positive IOD generally enhances
  monsoon rainfall over India
- Pacific Decadal Oscillation (PDO)
- Madden-Julian Oscillation (MJO): Controls active/break cycles

Active and Break Monsoon Periods:
- Active period: above-normal rainfall, strong winds
- Break period: below-normal rainfall, dry conditions over plains

Climate Change Impacts on Indian Monsoon:
- Increasing intensity of extreme rainfall events
- More variable onset and withdrawal dates
- Increasing frequency of floods and droughts
- Sea surface temperature changes affecting monsoon strength
